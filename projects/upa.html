The project

United People of Azerbaijan (UPA) is a community-resource prototype for Azerbaijanis living, studying, visiting, or building a new life in the United States. Its aim is to make useful information and community connections easier to find, starting with a detailed Illinois guide.

Why we started

Useful resources already exist, but people often need to move between websites and community groups to understand where to begin. UPA brings a clearer structure to that search: choose a location, find the category that matches your situation, and explore relevant information. The platform is intended to complement existing communities and organizations.

My contribution

  • Information structure: organized the prototype around state-based guides, nationwide information, and audience needs.
  • Development: built and refined the page structure, navigation, shared components, and responsive layouts using React, JavaScript, HTML, and CSS.
  • Illinois guide: developed the state overview and category-page layouts, including entry points for students and visitors.
  • Multilingual content: worked on English, Azerbaijani, and Russian content and the language-switching interface.
  • Iteration: adjusted spacing, typography, reusable styles, and mobile layouts as the prototype developed.

A walkthrough

Explore the prototype+
  • Start at the homepage. The introduction explains the platform and offers two starting points: Choose Your State and U.S.-Wide Information.
  • Explore Illinois. The state overview introduces the location, with Chicago imagery and a clear transition into local resources.
  • Choose a path. Categories such as Students and Visitors & Tourists help visitors find information relevant to their situation.
  • Explore practical resources. Short-term stay content covers choosing an area, planning total costs, reviewing stay requirements, and considering transportation.
  • Connect with the community. The directory groups community organizations, events, student and professional networks, and community support. A separate community section points visitors toward WhatsApp groups.

Design decisions

Visual identity & languages+

I used clear headings, repeatable cards, and shared page components to give the resource pages a consistent structure. Navy and light backgrounds establish the visual hierarchy, while blue, red, and green accents connect the interface to Azerbaijani identity. Language and responsive-layout work are part of making the same information approachable across different contexts. The community section appears in English, Azerbaijani, and Russian, with a matching layout and translated headings, descriptions, and buttons.

Current outcome

The prototype brings together Illinois resources, visitor-planning guidance, a community directory, and translated interface sections. It provides a foundation for community testing; the interactive U.S. map and expansion remain next steps.

What I learned

This project has helped me think beyond individual pages and focus on how people find their way through information. Changes to navigation, shared components, language, and mobile spacing affect the experience together. It also connects my development work with a community need I care about.

Next steps

Gather community feedback, verify the usefulness and accuracy of the Illinois resources, and test navigation, language behavior, and mobile layouts. Refine the guide using those findings before expanding to other states.
