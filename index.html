Strata Solutions · IT internship

Four GPUs are installed. Linux sees three.

Where would you look for the fourth card?

?
Read the Strata case study

Choose your first check

Compare the system and driver device lists

If Linux lists four GPUs and the NVIDIA driver lists three, I’d investigate driver detection. If both lists show three, I’d check how the hardware is being detected.

Review the startup logs

I’d look for a PCIe or NVIDIA driver error during startup. An error at that stage would narrow down where detection stopped.

Inspect the card and its connections

With the workstation powered down, I’d check the card’s seating and power connections. After startup, I’d compare the new device count with the original three.

What we checked at Strata

I started with Bash diagnostics, then inspected the GPU, cables, and connections with my team. We verified that Linux could see all four GPUs.

NASA Open Science 101 · Workshop research

Can these records tell us how wide a crater would be?

Our NASA catalog included these diameter and speed ranges.

Read the NASA case study

Choose your first check

Check which inputs a crater estimate needs

Our records gave us size and speed. Impact angle and composition were missing, so an estimate would depend on assumptions our data couldn’t establish.

Compare the smallest and largest asteroids

Comparing the extremes shows how widely sizes vary. To translate either diameter into a crater width, I’d still need to account for how the impact happens.

Map the close approaches

A map can help explain a hypothetical scenario to an audience. It doesn’t supply the missing impact conditions for a crater estimate.

How we changed the research question

After discussing the missing inputs with our advisor, we examined how size and speed affect estimated energy. I helped clean the parameters and compare the modeled energies in Python and Excel.

Tapia Conference · Four-hour hackathon

A student answers two questions about the same concept.

First attempt
Incorrect

Read the Tapia case study

Choose your first check

Use the overall quiz score

A total score tells me how many questions the student answered correctly. It loses the link between a particular mistake and the concept they could practice.

Keep a list of every missed question

That list remembers the first mistake. Without checking later attempts, it would keep this concept in practice even after the student answered it correctly.

Look up the latest answer for the concept

The concept ID connects the two attempts. The latest answer is correct, so this concept can drop off the missed-topics list.

How our study companion remembers

I worked on grading, quiz memory, and the Streamlit interface. We saved each concept ID with its result and timestamp. The practice screen uses the latest result for each concept to pick questions from the current quiz.
